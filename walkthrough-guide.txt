# Hunt the Wumpus -- how to hunt
#
# Your cave is not this cave. The wumpus, the pit and the room you wake in are
# drawn fresh for every game, so the five moves at the end will not be your five
# moves. What carries over is the method. The worked hunt is there so you can
# watch the method being used once; the transcript beside it is that same game.

You wake somewhere in the middle of twenty-five rooms with one arrow and no way
out. Somewhere down here is the wumpus. Somewhere else is a pit that has no
bottom worth measuring. You cannot see either of them coming, and walking into
either one ends the game on the spot.

You are not exploring. You are collecting evidence.

## Reading the walls -- the only two things the cave tells you

Every room reports itself as you enter it, and two of those reports are the
entire game:

Blood on the walls means the wumpus is **next door** -- in one of the rooms at
the far end of this room's tunnels. Not in this room. If it were in this room
you would already know.

Grey slime means the pit is next door, on exactly the same terms.

A room can show both. A room can show neither, and that is evidence too --
possibly the best evidence you will get.

What neither one ever tells you is **which** tunnel. That is the whole problem,
and no amount of staring at the walls will fix it. The answer comes from
standing somewhere else and comparing.

## The one rule -- never walk in to check

The temptation, every single time, is to step into the suspect room and settle
it. Do not. There is no "check" in this game: entering the wumpus's room is
being eaten, and entering the pit is falling into it. You find out by being
right, not by looking.

Anything you want to know, you can work out from rooms you have already survived.

## Working it out -- how the deduction actually goes

Three facts do all the work.

A room you have stood in is **proven safe**. It holds neither hazard. You can
walk back through it as often as you like.

A room with blood tells you the wumpus is in one of *its* neighbours -- usually
two to five candidates. That is your suspect list.

A clean room is the strong move. No blood means the wumpus is in **none** of
that room's neighbours, which crosses several rooms off at once. Hunters lose
because they only pay attention to the rooms that frighten them.

So: find blood, then go and stand somewhere that shares only *part* of that
room's neighbourhood. Whatever both readings agree on is where it is. Two
readings usually settle it. Three always have, in my experience.

Use the tunnel list at the bottom of every room to draw the cave on paper as you
go. Five across and five down, but not every wall is open and some of the ways
through go cornerwise, so the shape will surprise you. If you lose your bearings:

# MAP does not draw you a map -- nobody down here has one. It tells you how well
# you know the room you are standing in: first time, been here once, or been
# through often enough to walk it in the dark.

## Taking the shot -- you get one

An arrow goes down a tunnel without you, which is the only reason any of this is
survivable. Stand in a room you trust and shoot along the tunnel you want:
SHOOT NORTH, SHOOT SOUTHEAST.

An arrow only goes where a tunnel goes, and it keeps going while the tunnel runs
straight -- up to four rooms down a long gallery. So you do not have to be next
door to kill it. Sight-lines are worth as much as adjacency, and they are worth
working out before you need them.

You have one arrow. If it does not find him, he finds you. There is no second
attempt and no walking away.

## A hunt, worked through -- one real game, start to finish

What follows is a complete hunt. In this cave the wumpus was in the Drum and the
pit was in the Cistern, and I woke in the Shambles -- yours will differ.

Read the note first. It is the rules, in the hand of somebody who did not make
it out, and it costs nothing.

> read note

Now move. The Shambles was clean -- no blood, no slime -- so both hazards are at
least two rooms away, and both tunnels out are safe to take.

> west

The Dead Well: clean again. Four tunnels, and every one of its neighbours is now
ruled out. Keep going.

> south

The Rookery: clean. Three more rooms crossed off, and note that the Gullet is one
of them -- the Rookery being clean proves the Gullet holds nothing. That will
matter in about ten seconds.

> west

The Gullet, and blood on the walls. The wumpus is in one of the Gullet's
neighbours, and the Gullet has exactly two: the Rookery and the Drum. I walked
out of the Rookery thirty seconds ago, so it is not that.

There is only one room left it can be. I never went and looked.

> shoot north

## Ways this ends -- four of them, and only one is good

You kill it. The arrow finds something that is not rock, and the cave becomes
just a cave.

It eats you. You walked into its room, which is what blood on the walls was
trying to prevent.

The pit takes you. Same mistake, quieter ending.

It finds you first. You spent the arrow on the wrong room, and the sound of it
striking bare rock told the wumpus exactly where you were standing.
